There's been a lot of debate on health lately. Everyone seems to have become expert epidemiologists, sociologists, statisticians, immunologists, and nutritionists. There are the "vaxxers" and "antivaxxers", the "Independent news" and "Mainstream Media", and everyone seems to be talking past each other.

Of course, everyone thinks that their cause is noble: to reveal the truth to the poor sheeple that are on the other side.

Here's a time saver for you: There is no article, blog post, podcast or youtube video that will convince the person to abandon ship and come to your life raft of truth and reason.

Why? **Because there is an underlying philosophy that needs to be addressed.**

I'm not going to try to advocate one side or the other, but I'd like to explore two different philosophies that have been clashing lately: the Integrated and the Systems philosophies.

I'm just making these terms up, even though there's probably actual terms out there, because they're already loaded in your mind. Let me try to define these two philosophies as best as possible. When I define them, I want to define them in ways that people from either side would agree with — I'm not interested in building up strawmen.

## Integrated Philosophy

This is the person that talks about "building up the immune system". They look for "natural" remedies, and are deeply suspicious of "traditional medicine". The term "Big Pharma" is used derisively in this crowd.

The basic idea is this: **The body knows best**.

For those that come from a theistic background, it's because God has created the body with all of the tools it needs to fight off any health threat.

For those that come from an evolutionistic background, it's because humans are the result of billions of years of evolution, perfectly fine-tuned to adapt to any kind of threat.

The Integrated Philosophy sees the human being as a unit of irreducible complexity, that is connected to the rest of the universe through nature — that is why natural remedies are best, since they work with the existing connections between nature and human.

The Integrated philosophy sees things like vaccines as an interruption of the natural order. God/Evolution/Whatever created a way for humans to interact with the natural world, and sticking a needle into your body hijacks that way of working, and sets off a chain reaction of problems inside of the human body.

## Systems Philosophy

A person that follows Systems Philosophy is most likely connected to a traditional system of education & healthcare. 

The basic idea is this: **It's systems all the way down.**

The body is made of organs, which are made of cells, which are made of proteins which are made of chemicals. They can be studied, tweaked, and fixed.

The scientific method works, and it needs to be followed and respected. Systems Philosophy understands the human body as many different systems that can be understood. Through science and technology, we've identified the parts of humans that can break down, and by taking research to the molecular level, we can create treatments for whatever is ailing the person. There are no atheists in foxholes, and there are no homeopaths with exposed fractures.

Systems philosophy sees things like vaccines like a scientific solution to the chaotic world of disease and infection. Plumbing cured Cholera, and vaccines eliminated Polio. Human advancement can be messy and take wrong turns, but in the long run, we progress as a species when we learn about the systems that exist in the body, identify the breakpoints, and work to solve it.

## What's the point of this?

How'd I do? Do you fall into one of the camps? Did I describe it accurately?

It's important to understand that for any issue, there is a person on the other side that has thought out the issue, and come to a conclusion. The first thing that needs to happen is that you have to **Listen, **not for the goal of responding, but to be able to **Understand** where the other person is coming from. Try to get an idea of the basic philosophy behind the argument. From there, move to **Empathizing**, - "I can see how, if you see the world in that way, how you'd come to the conclusion you did." Finally, and only after having passed those three steps, will ANYTHING you have to say make any difference in the mind of the other person. That's when you **talk**.

So, let's stop fooling ourselves into thinking that our post or article share is going to "wake up the sheeple", and let's start asking the "sheeple" what they think, and why they think the way they do. "Do YoUr ReSeArCH" doesn't quite cut it either. The research will be accepted or rejected based on the underlying philosophy they've come to believe, and until that's not addressed, there will never be a change.
