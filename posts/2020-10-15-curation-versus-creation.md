Neil Postman, comparing George Orwell's dystopian world of "1984" and the utopian "Brave New World" of Aldous Huxley, said in his book, "Amusing Ourselves to Death":

> Orwell feared that the truth would be concealed from us. Huxley feared the truth would be drowned in a sea of irrelevance. Orwell feared we would become a captive culture. Huxley feared we would become a trivial culture, preoccupied with some equivalent of the feelies, the orgy porgy, and the centrifugal bumble puppy.

The internet brought us a tidal wave of information.

- 500 hours of video content are uploaded to youtube every minute.

- There are over [1.7 billion](https://www.oberlo.com/blog/internet-statistics#:~:text=Here's%20a%20summary%20of%20the,percent%20of%20the%20global%20population.) websites on the world wide web.

- [3.5 billion](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/number-of-internet-users-by-country?tab=chart&time=1990..2016&country=~OWID_WRL) humans use the internet

- People are connected an average of [6 hours per day](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/daily-hours-spent-with-digital-media-per-adult-user)

With so many people online consuming content, the amount of content generated has become staggering. We are flooded by information on the web. It's all there, and for the most part, free.

It seems as though we're living in Huxley's "Brave New World". With so much information, it becomes overwhelming.

As an entrepreneur, it's a double whammy - sometimes it feels like everything that's been created has already been created, and everything interesting you may have to say has already been said.

And it's probably true. 

- There's already [top tier classes](https://online.stanford.edu/free-courses) online that you can take for free

- There is already an incredible amount of interviews, books, articles from great minds around the world putting out higher quality content than you'll ever hope to attain.

- There are already [amazing creators](https://levels.io/) building tools that will make everyone's life easier.

With all this talent, you might as well sit down, shut up and let the professionals work their magic.

What is someone supposed to do?

**Curation.**

Defined as "*the action or process of selecting, organizing, and looking after the items in a collection or exhibition*", curation is always going to be needed, and will be even more needed as content grows. As a curator, you have a chance to go through a vast collection of things, and present it to people in a way that lets them focus **just on what they need**.

# Curation will always be in demand

There's a reason celebrities get paid millions of dollars to put out a tweet recommending one product or another. It's because that person has created a huge following of people that trust his or her opinion. "Well, if this product is good enough for X celebrity, it's good enough for me". They've made that celebrity a kind of curator.

[https://www.goodbooks.io/](https://www.goodbooks.io/) is a great example of a website designed to curate book choices. Who is doing the curating? According to the website, it's "successful people". Who is curating the "successful people"? The web developer.

Is the developer doing anything new, groundbreaking, incredible? Not really. It's a nice-looking, polished site, but there's not really anything new in terms of CONTENT. However, he is providing a service: putting all the book recommendation from "successful people" in one searchable page. And as a reader, that has enormous value.

# Curation has never been easier

That website I just mentioned was built using webflow, a drag-and-drop website creator. Today, with modern tools, you can build out a useful tool to other people, just by pointing them to useful resources. This can take the form of an e-book, a web page, or even an [email newsletter](https://thewritelife.com/earn-money-through-substack/).

You don't need to be a web developer, a professional coder, or even a good writer. Anyone in a position to know **a little more** than the average person is able to put together a list of resources that cuts to the chase, gets past the noise, and guides others to where they want to go.

# Curation is as much a service as creation

A museum curator is never going to be applauded as much as the artist. However, the curator knew how to put the artist's work in a context that would delight and inform the museumgoer. The curator tells a story, and it's as much of a service as the person that created the work. By curating, you can put things in a useful context, and allow others to get right to the best parts, without having to slog through the petabytes of useless, rehashed crap that can be found on the internet.

**Creators offer novelty. Curators offer clarity.**
