In engineering, there are six "simple machines". They're defined as "as the simplest mechanisms that use mechanical advantage (also called leverage) to multiply force." They are:

- [Lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever)

- [Wheel and axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_and_axle)

- [Pulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley)

- [Inclined plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_plane)

- [Wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_(mechanical_device))

- [Screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_(simple_machine))

With these simple machines, you can easily become more efficient in your work. Combining them makes them even more efficient

## Internet Simple Machines

At its most basic level, the internet connects things. There are basic categories to connection needs that can be combined to create even better online tools. They're not really "simple machines" in a mechanical sense — think of them more like robots (or [demons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemon_(computing))). Although there might be more, i've defined the internet's simple machines as the following: 

## The Bouncer

Access control. This guy decides who comes in and goes out based on the conditions you gave it.

It's basically a login.

## The Scout

This is information retrieval and collection. It's the thing that hits an API for the weather and displays it. It's the stock ticker.

## The Number Cruncher

The Number Cruncher is a computer in a classical sense — something that computes.

It's the little form that converts Celsius to Fahrenheit, the thing that tells you [if it's Christmas](https://isitchristmas.com/).

## The Messenger

It facilitates connection between two people. Could be a contact form, a chatroom — whatever. Anything that makes two people able to communicate more efficiently.

Combinations of these things create value.

For example, a marketplace like Amazon is a communication tool, a retrieval tool and an access tool. With an account (bouncer), you get a listout of vendors (scout), and can transact with them (messenger).

### What about exchanging money?

Buying and selling online is an extremely complicated process, so a lot of it has been black-boxed by companies that facilitate payments, like Stripe, Paypal and Gumroad. It looks simple to us, but behind the scenes there's a lot going on. In fact, a lot of what we're doing when we set up a payment portal is building a scout to do an API call to our favorite service, and having the number cruncher calculate prices on our end, and send that information along. Then the rules will be passed to the Bouncer to give access to whatever we had behind our paywall, or passed to a Messenger to alert the seller that a sale has completed.

### Why does this matter?

To the user, this isn't very important. But as a creator, I want to explore new frameworks and languages beyond simple tutorials that take me down a set path of how to build a todo app or whatever. By learning how to make these four machines, I can instantly get value out of what I'm building, just like engineers can get value immediately from implementing simple machines into what they're building. So by understanding how access control works, how data collection works, how business logic and communication works, I'll have the building blocks for my next project.
