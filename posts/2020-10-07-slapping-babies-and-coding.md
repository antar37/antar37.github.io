Newborn babies look incredibly fragile, and any kind of movement feels like they would break. Parents are very gentle with their newborn babies, mostly because they don't want to do anything that would hurt their tiny baby.

Compare that with the doctors, nurses and attendants that handle babies all day long. They'll grab the baby by the legs, slap it on the butt, move it around without too much of a problem, and handle the child in ways parents wouldn't dream of doing.

To the untrained eye, it might look like carelessness. Don't they know that you have to be careful around babies?! However, they're being extremely careful. *They just know what matters and what doesn't.*

Babies, for example have weak necks, but a relatively strong spine. You can move them around by the legs and even hang them upside down, because as long as you don't mess with their neck, they're ok. Full term babies' skin is much more resilient than premature babies' skin. So while you would limit your handling of a preemie, you shouldn't have too much of a problem with a full-term baby.

To parents, however EVERYTHING IS FRAGILE PROCEED WITH CAUTION.

## Computers & Coding

This behavior isn't limited to new parents. I used to have lots of fun with my grandmother's computer, clearing cache, deleting unused folders, and stopping certain programs from starting up on boot, while she stood behind me, white-knuckled and saying "BE CAREFUL DON'T BREAK ANYTHING".

Web development is all about learning how to interact with the code in a way to get the results you want. An unexperienced person will look through a tutorial or article, and think that if they don't put the code in the EXACT order they found it, they'll break something. But someone who has gotten his hands dirty and experimented with the code knows what's important and what isn't.

- Does the order of the properties in an HTML tag matter? Not really — feel free to swap 'em around.

- Does the order of the values in a CSS shorthand property matter? Yes, so be careful.

As you get better and feel more comfortable working in the code, you'll learn that it's OK to move things around — you're not going to break anything (and if you do, hopefully you can start over). However, it's important to make the distinction: the goal isn't to become careless, it's to learn what things need extra care, and what things don't.
