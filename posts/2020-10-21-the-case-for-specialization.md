I once lost out on a web project because "the other company that quoted us specializes in websites for franchises".

*What?*

"This is an information website. The only difference is content - a website is a website", I thought. "There's no difference between a 'franchise' website and any other kind of website!"

It's as ridiculous as an architect saying that he "specializes" in building houses for families with small children. 

A house is a house — the only difference is the number of rooms! Whether it's for children or not depends on how you furnish it.

That was a few years ago. With time, I've come to respect my mysterious competition. They understood how to position themselves as unique in a field where there isn't much difference between one kind of product and another (in my case, websites). Although their claim to "specialization" seemed to be marketing fluff in order to close the deal, there are very legitimate reasons to put yourself out in the market as a "specialist" in a specific demographic. 

Here are three:

# You can anticipate specific needs

If our hypothetical architect started getting lots and lots of jobs from families with small children, he may begin to anticipate some requests.

"I'm pretty sure you're going to want a good-sized backyard. Do you want me to factor that into the blueprints?"

"Most of my clients ask for a central kitchen that has access to most parts of the house. Would you like that as well?"

Every niche has specific needs. As someone who has built several websites for church and para-church organizations, I can anticipate certain needs they have, such as the need for accessible audio files, a home page with access to basic information like service times, location and current sermon series. I can even anticipate the kinds of problems they're going to have as the project progresses.

Do I code any differently, or is the actual work any different than if my client were in another industry? 

No.

*What's the benefit, then?*

The client benefits by my specialization and past experience because apart from getting a developer, he's also getting a consultant who can inform him about any blind spots he may have. This can be extremely valuable in the long run, and may even allow a "specialized" developer to charge a premium for that additional expertise.

# You can one-up the industry while staying in-budget

Companies do not all compete with one another. Companies in the same industry compete with one another. In the same way, websites don't have to compete with all the websites that exist, they have to keep up with their competition, and hopefully surpass it.

Having a good understanding of a client's competition allows you to build a solution that lets your client to stand out in their field, while relieving them of the pressure to feel like they need a website that looks like [apple.com](http://apple.com).

It's important to strive for excellence, but if the client is constrained by budget, a good understanding of the industry can give the the insight you need to know what "standing out" will require.

# You can become a point of reference

The pool guy is in touch with the pool supply company, who talks to pool noodle manufacturers. If the pool guy needs help with web design and online marketing, you can become the point of reference in that little business ecosystem, especially if there are concrete sales results attached with your work. By positioning yourself as a "specialist" in pool-related web design and marketing, you give your client a reason to recommend your work to other people in the industry. If we're really honest, it's not exactly a true specialization (since the process isn't different), but by anticipating needs and keeping your clients a step ahead of their own competition, you can become a reference point to everyone associated with that particular client. This, in turn, will reinforce your aura of "expertise".

So see what slice of your potential market you understand best, and lean into it. Invent a specialization for yourself — who knows, it might just pay off.
