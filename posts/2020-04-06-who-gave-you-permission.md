One of the things that stop people from writing is the idea of permission - A little idea in the back of your head that you're not "allowed" to write about a given topic, because you're not qualified, certified, or recognized. "**You don't belong**", is what the gatekeepers are saying.

### The most intimidating gatekeeper: yourself.

Most of the time no one needs to be guarding the door to a given topic or community, because it's the little voice in your head that's doing the hard work of making you feel disqualified.

The problem of qualification is that in order to produce quality work, you have to first make horrible work. There's really no way around it. Sometimes that horrible work happens in a closed environment (like the case of a university student learning how to design. Their terrible work is hidden behind the university walls), or it's done in the open (like a cover band that performs their terrible music in friends-and-family concerts). In my case, I'm dedicating myself to write in the open, and share it.

I'm reminded of this [quote](http://deltaskymag.com/Sky-Extras/Favorites/Rock--n--Roll-Jedi.aspx) by Dave Grohl:

> When I think about kids watching a TV show like *American Idol *or *The Voice*, then they think, ‘Oh, OK, that’s how you become a musician, you stand in line for eight f****** hours with 800 people at a convention center and then you sing your heart out for someone and then they tell you it’s not f****** good enough.’ Can you imagine?” he implores. “It’s destroying the next generation of musicians! Musicians should go to a yard sale and buy an old f****** drum set and get in their garage and just suck. And get their friends to come in and they’ll suck, too. And then they’ll f******* start playing and they’ll have the best time they’ve ever had in their lives and then all of a sudden they’ll become Nirvana. Because that’s exactly what happened with Nirvana. Just a bunch of guys that had some s***** old instruments and they got together and started playing some noisy-a** s***, and they became the biggest band in the world. That can happen again! You don’t need a f****** computer or the Internet or *The Voice *or *American Idol*.”

### Other gatekeepers: your competition

When do other people start to try to stop you from creating? When you start to get good enough to be a threat. That's when others will start nitpicking and taking apart your work, and try to get you to "stay in your lane". In a sense, it's a good thing. When people are actively trying to close the door on you, it's because you already got your foot in.

### The only real gatekeepers: your consumers

J.K Rowling, the only billionaire book author, got rejected by 12 publishing houses. She only got the green light when the 8-year-old daughter of a publishing house chairman read the first chapter and demanded the next one. 

So who are you creating for? Those are the people you answer to. If your consumers close the door on you, there's nothing else you can do.

As for that little voice in your head, don't shut it out completely. "This is terrible, you're not any good", it says. And that voice is most likely right. But it's also pointing to something else: that you know what you want, and you're not there yet.

Listen to this essay by Ira Glass (it's a famous one, so you've probably heard it before) on the gap between what you want, and what you're able to create in the moment.

[https://www.youtube.com/embed/X2wLP0izeJE](https://www.youtube.com/embed/X2wLP0izeJE)

What do you do with the external gatekeepers? Pay attention to them, too. But pay attention to the motive: The more intense the pushback becomes, the more of a threat you're becoming - so use their resistance as signposts.

So who gave you permission? No one. You have to give it to yourself, and get out there and make something crappy. Make it in private, make it in public, but understand that if you're not making something horrible, you'll never make something great.
