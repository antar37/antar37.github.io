Something happens every time I want to do something consistently — especially writing. I've got some ideas in the back of my pocket, and I want to start to put out content into the world.

And then, I run out of things to say. I don't want to write anymore.

I'm not the only one - It's actually a thing in startup world. They call it "The trough of sorrow". It's when the buzz and the initial enthusiasm wears off, and you realize that success is only going to come by checking in, doing the work, and being consistent.

[https://twitter.com/AdamJTPalmer/status/459688087178579968?s=20](https://twitter.com/AdamJTPalmer/status/459688087178579968?s=20)

No one likes to struggle through a routine that isn't fun anymore. No one likes to be intentional with something that doesn't captivate and enchant the way it used to. No one wants to put in the hours.

But everyone wants to have a book with their name on it.

Everyone wants to have done "something great".

Everyone wants that sculpted body, that sharp mind, that incredible skill.

Everyone wants the result of the hard work without actually doing it.

### So what do I do?

I don't know — I'm just writing this because I didn't post yesterday's until today! Go read a motivational blog, I'm asking the same questions you are, buddy.

In all seriousness — two things: One, go read a [post I wrote a while ago about habits](https://medium.com/@antar37/i-should-do-that-thing-more-4edc212cca71). I'm still struggling with this, so it's as much a reminder to me as it is a help to you.

Second, know that when your highest priority is consistency, you're not going to hit home runs every time. Some days it's going to be a bad workout, a bad article, a disappointing practice. That's OK, because you showed up, and did the work.

And for that, I applaud you.
