First, a story about tractor hacking:

Apparently farmers that bought relatively modern tractors found themselves out of options when it came to repairs, because John Deere made it so that any repair had to be done by authorized John Deere technicians.

Which creates a question: when do you really own something? If there are things you can't do with your own property, is it really yours?

I don't want to get into the nuance about it, but it's safe to say that restrictions make something "less yours". You're not "sovereign" over the thing you supposedly own.

### Black Boxes

A "black box" is any part of a system that is obscured. You basically see it in terms of inputs and outputs. I give the black box something, and something spits out.

Sometimes it's intentionally obscured, like anything with a "secret recipe" (it's called "special sauce" because you have no idea what's in it).

Sometimes it's obscured because you just have no idea what's going on, like me whenever I pop the hood of my trunk. I give the car gas, and it lets me drive. The engine is pretty much a big black box to me. Input and output.

Black boxes create dependencies. Farmers need a John Deere tech to work on their tractor, even though they know how to do it. I need a mechanic to work on my car, because I don't know what I'm doing. 

### What does this have to do with a badly built blog?

I've used different blogging platforms, but I wanted to take ownership of what I was putting together. On Medium, you're putting your articles on a distribution service. They can take executive decisions regarding the platform, and they don't need your permission. 

On WordPress, the actual post sits on a WordPress-formatted database table entry. Moving, exporting, or changing platforms can get difficult. Also, WordPress is a system that I already know and understand. I wanted the challenge of getting my hands dirty in a react-based JS environment.

I wanted to intentionally do the hard thing of working on every aspect of the system, in order to know how it's all put together. In order to make it *really* mine.

I want to figure out how Gatsby's platform works, how routing is set up, how the markdown files are set up to display, how far I can stretch Github's free "pages" platform.

Because at the end of the day, the only thing you really own, the only thing no one can take away from you, are the lessons you learn.
